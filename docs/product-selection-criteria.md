# Watch the Night — Product Selection Criteria

How we decide what gets featured. This doc is the rulebook; every product on the site must pass it.

## 1. Niche filter

The site exists inside the portfolio's durable filter: the niche must have **people already buying products** in it AND **an existing audience or Hector's own knowledge**. Citizen skywatch gear passes both: amateur astronomers already buy telescopes, binoculars, and cameras — and RealUFO's audience is already asking "what was that light?", ready to look up and verify it themselves.

## 2. Audience-first, not gear-first

Positioning: **"Look up. Verify it yourself."** We serve curious people becoming observers, not the existing hardcore astro community (plenty of sites do that). Products must match one of the three buyer segments:

1. **Curious first-time binocular buyers** — "I just want to look at the sky tonight."
2. **Photographers adding a star tracker** — "I already own a camera; what unlocks the night sky?"
3. **Gift buyers buying a first telescope** — "I need something good but not overwhelming."

If a product doesn't clearly serve at least one segment, it doesn't get a page — no matter how good the affiliate terms are.

## 3. Community-vetted products only

Featured gear must be **established, widely recommended products with years of community track record** — the "usual suspects" that show up in every credible "best beginner telescope" discussion. Rules:

- No junk, no no-name Amazon brands, no hype launch products without a proven reputation.
- No pay-to-rank: affiliate availability never determines whether a product gets featured.
- If a product gets a deserved reputation change (e.g. quality slips, a better alternative arrives), its page gets updated — credibility is the asset.

## 4. A full price ladder per category

Every category should span **entry → sweet spot → premium**, so every visitor finds something honest at their budget:

- **Telescopes:** AstroMaster 70AZ (~$150) → Heritage 150P (~$300) → StarSense DX 130AZ (~$480) → Apertura AD8 (~$700) → NexStar 6SE (~$1,199) → NexStar 8SE (~$1,589)
- **Binoculars:** Cometron 7x50 (~$40) → Nature DX 8x42 (~$150) → Aculon 10x50 (~$120) → Diamondback HD 10x42 (~$230)
- **Camera gear:** EF 50mm f/1.8 STM (~$125) → NexYZ adapter (~$65) → Star Adventurer 2i (~$430) → SkyGuider Pro (~$479) → EOS Rebel T7 (~$550)
- **Accessories:** $15 red flashlight → Telrad (~$45) → planisphere (~$15) → Barlow / Cheshire / books (~$30–130)

We'd rather recommend "start cheaper than you planned" than push anyone up the ladder.

## 5. Honest pros AND cons on every page

Every product page carries both a "Why we like it" list and a "Keep in mind" list. Cons must be real (mount wobble, needs collimation, heavy, you'll outgrow it). We also recommend against over-buying where appropriate (start with binoculars before a telescope). No sponsored placements, no rankings sold to vendors.

## 6. Approximate "around $X" pricing

Prices render as **"around $X"**, never as live quotes. They are approximate US street prices, re-verified periodically against retailer pages and refreshed when materially wrong (roughly quarterly or when noticed). If a price can't be verified, the page says "Check price" instead of guessing.

## 7. Product fit first, affiliate availability second

Affiliate programs are a monetization layer, not a selection criterion. Until a specialist retailer program is joined, retailer links render as **"Link coming soon"** placeholders (FTC-compliant, no dead links). Amazon search links use the site's affiliate tag where available. A product is never featured because its commission is attractive.

## 8. Compliance is non-negotiable

- **FTC disclosure** on the site and near buy boxes.
- All affiliate links carry **rel="nofollow sponsored"**.
- JSON-LD Product/Offer markup uses the approximate price honestly (as `around`, not a promise).
